# Curriculum specification

**Status:** Accepted baseline  
**Initial track:** German A2–B1 for Software Developer Interviews  
**Nominal duration:** 21 days

## 1. Model

```text
Course → Track → Module → Lesson → Learning objective → Activity blueprint
                                             ↓
                                      Content versions
```

“Day 2” is a plan slot, not permanent content identity. Lessons may be reused in other days, tracks, and reviews.

## 2. Learning stages

Important targets move through Encounter, Recognize, Controlled Production, Contextual Production, Spoken Production, Interview Transfer, and Retention. Completing one activity is not mastery.

## 3. Daily template

A nominal 60–90 minute day includes 5–10 minutes review, 10–15 new vocabulary, 10 grammar, 15–20 controlled exercises, 10–15 answer construction, 10–20 speaking, and a 5-minute exit test.

Sessions may be split and checkpointed. Opening pages does not count as completion.

## 4. Twenty-one-day plan

### Days 1–3: Professional foundation
Self-introduction, current situation, goals, experience, education, core modal language, project basics, present and intention forms.

**Output:** 60-second introduction and simple project description.

### Days 4–6: Contribution and delivery
Responsibilities, implementation, integration, testing, Perfekt patterns, team versus individual contribution, delivery and outcomes.

**Output:** credible personal-contribution explanation.

### Days 7–10: Architecture and reasoning
Components, flow, APIs, databases, frontend/backend, Docker, cloud, CI/CD, monitoring, technology choice, trade-offs, scalability, and causal connectors.

**Output:** architecture explanation at two depths.

### Days 11–14: Problems and behavior
Debugging, incidents, challenge-action-result, conflict, feedback, prioritization, strengths, weakness, failure, and learning.

**Output:** two reusable truthful behavioral stories.

### Days 15–17: Role depth
Backend/database, frontend, DevOps/cloud, AI/computer vision, security, testing, production operations, and follow-ups.

**Output:** concise answers across target role families.

### Days 18–20: Interview performance
Mixed mock interviews, clarification, time-boxing, weak-area review, learner questions, and reduced scaffolding.

**Output:** two comparable full mock interviews.

### Day 21: Final validation
Review warm-up, final realistic interview, baseline comparison, readiness report, and continuation plan.

## 5. Initial 100 verbs

Days 1–14 introduce seven per day (98). Day 15 introduces two and consolidates. Days 16–21 emphasize production and review. Curriculum assigns precise targets rather than a raw list.

## 6. Question taxonomy

Introduction, motivation, strengths/development, teamwork/conflict, challenge/failure, project/responsibility, architecture/trade-off, backend/API/database, frontend, DevOps/cloud, AI/vision, testing/security, logistics, and learner questions.

Stable taxonomy codes enable coverage measurement.

## 7. Scaffolding

- Level 0: translation, grammar, structure, full model.
- Level 1: keywords and structure.
- Level 2: question, time, optional keywords.
- Level 3: question only.
- Level 4: adaptive follow-up.

Scaffolding changes with evidence, not only day number.

## 8. Personalization

Personalization selects examples, project facts, domains, weak targets, and order. It never forks unmaintainable course copies. It combines canonical objectives, reusable blueprints, verified learner claims, and stored generated instances.

## 9. Unlock and completion

Unlock may depend on previous completion, prerequisite evidence, calendar, diagnostic skip, or editor override. Mastery must not block continuation.

Day completion requires required submissions, exit check, and speaking completion or explicit deferral. Platform-owned processing failure cannot punish the learner.

## 10. Assessments

Baseline and final assessments use comparable but different tasks: introduction, project, behavior, technical explanation, recovery, and controlled language. A versioned rubric separates language, structure, and relevance and reports uncertainty.

## 11. Releases

Drafts support reorder, substitution, estimates, prerequisites, optional activities, workload preview, coverage matrix, duplicate detection, and migration simulation. Publish creates a pinned manifest.

## 12. Quality gates

All references resolve; no forbidden cycles; daily load is acceptable; category coverage is visible; renderers/evaluators are compatible; mobile preview works; translations exist; rubrics are pinned; personalized claims are verified; release diff is reviewed.
